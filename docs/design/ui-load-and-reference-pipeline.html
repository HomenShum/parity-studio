What the interface asks for, and where the reference pipeline stops

Two questions, one answer each. Which UI complexity can go — measured, not guessed. And whether a graph helps the meta-process — it does, in exactly one place, and that place is currently blocked on a sign-in.

1. The measurement

“The design tab is confusing, too much to read, too technical” was a judgement. Judgements cannot be tracked across releases. This is the same statement as a number, taken from the deployed build by tools/brain/measure-ui-load.mjs.

The Design tab is not busier. It is wordier. Inside the inspector column it asks for 20.9 words of reading per available action, against 6.4 on the AI tab — 3.3×. Nobody skips a tab because it has too many buttons; they skip it because it reads like documentation.

The four-size type scale holds in one column and nowhere else. It is exactly 4 inside .ns-inspector-scroll. Discounting the sub-pixel sizes produced by scaled slide thumbnails, the rest of the application still renders 11 distinct chrome sizes, including 9px and 10.5px. The scale shipped to the column it was written for and was reported as shipped.

2. What to remove, in order

Each is tied to the measurement that justifies it. Anything I could not measure is not on this list.

01
Cut the Design tab’s prose, not its controls
It has 8 inspector controls and 167 inspector words. Halving the words would bring it to the AI tab’s ratio without removing a single capability. Explanatory sentences in persistent chrome are read once and occupy space forever — the product design contract already forbids them, and this is where the rule is being broken hardest.
20.9 words/control → target 6.4 · no control removed
02
Extend the type scale past the inspector
11 chrome sizes outside one column. The landing page alone still has 10px in 181 places. This is hierarchy asserted by shrinking rather than by colour, which the contract names as a defect. It is not a regex job: 21 hardcoded 10px and 15 hardcoded 11px declarations need a per-surface pass.
11 sizes → 4 · 9px and 10.5px eliminated
03
Split the Evidence tab’s 46 controls into a summary and a detail
The heaviest surface in the product: 110 page controls and 1,118 words. The job there is “check what backs this claim”, which is a reading task, and 46 simultaneous affordances is a triage task placed in front of it. Surface the verdict first; put the bindings behind it.
46 inspector controls · 477 words · heaviest measured step
04
Make the three unreachable states reachable — or delete them from the contract
exception, conflict and failed_safe cannot be reached on the deployed product. Two of them were scoring as reached until the sensors were unwelded today. Either the states are real and the UI must be able to show them, or the contract is asking for states this product does not have. Both are fixes; pretending is not.
required-state coverage 6/9 · exit 3
05
The popover that cannot be closed
At 1512×812 the advanced-controls popover renders beneath the toolbar. Its close button is present and enabled but pointer-intercepted, and Escape does not dismiss. This is not complexity to simplify — it is a trap, and it outranks every taste question on this page.
filed · needs a test that asserts dismissal, not presence

3. Where a graph actually helps

Not everywhere. Most of this work is a list, and a list drawn as boxes is a worse list. A graph earns its place where the failure is a missing edge rather than a missing node — where each piece exists, and what is broken is the handoff between them. That is precisely the reference pipeline: nearly every stage is built, and it produces nothing, because two edges are missing and one is legally constrained.

4. The three blockers, and which are yours

Sign in to Mobbin — but it unblocks less than the plan assumes.
The MCP server is registered and unauthorised, and this session cannot run an OAuth flow, so authorise it from claude.ai → Settings → Connectors (or claude mcp in an interactive terminal). That enables live lookup. It does not enable a stored corpus, because your own approved source policy forbids one.
The licence that already exists in your repo settles the corpus question.
shared/nodeslideAtlasRegistry.ts:512-536 — the mobbin source policy, reviewed by you on 2026-07-22 and marked approved:
download: false · cache: false · ragIndexing: false · embeddingIndex: false
displayThumbnail: true · accessMode: 'remote-mcp'
attributionRequired: true · competitiveUseRestricted: true
So Mobbin content may be displayed live, with attribution, and never downloaded, cached, or indexed. A 50-asset Mobbin corpus in the repo is not a licensing risk to be managed — it is already forbidden by a policy you approved, and the Atlas gate enforces it.
So “50-asset seed corpus” has to mean 50 records, from the two sources that permit storage.
Only nodeslide-owned and workspace-private carry cache: true. Everything else — Mobbin, Uiverse, commercial template libraries — is look-up only. The corpus is therefore 50 annotation records: source URL, archetype, and the named observation that makes it worth keeping. That is the more useful artifact regardless, because a model can retrieve an observation and cannot retrieve a screenshot.
The benchmark cannot be produced by the agent that made the change.
criticIndependent is a schema constant of true. An agent that prompts its own reviewer, on its own model, in its own session, cannot honestly assert it. The render half is produced today and the benchmark deliberately is not. This is structural, not a task — it needs a second party, and no amount of engineering removes that.

5. The meta-loop the graph is really for

The reason to draw this at all: every failure today was an edge, not a node. The importer existed and was not merged. The gates existed and pointed at a surface that had moved. The share route existed and could not resolve its own imports. The dashboard existed and measured a different repository. In each case every box was green and the arrow between two of them was broken.

The dotted edges are the two lessons. Green locally proves nothing about the deployed artifact — a socket-level test measured 14,930 bytes of correct output while the deployed function returned 500 to every request. And measurement has to feed back into deciding, or it becomes a number nobody acts on: the UI-load figures above only matter if the next design decision has to beat them.
